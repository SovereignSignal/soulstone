SURVIVAL READINESS
The survival observation reports server hunger (0–20), saturation, gamemode, difficulty, carried ordinary foods and nearby potentially dangerous entities. Unknown fields are unknown, never evidence of safety. Nearby entities are not necessarily aggressive and coverage is limited to loaded entities within 24 blocks. Check health, food and threats before long travel or construction; re-observe between bounded actions. Prioritize food and a verified safe shelter when hungry or hurt. Health does not regenerate at all below hunger 18: waiting, sheltering and digging in protect the agent but restore nothing, so hurt with hunger under 18 and no carried food is a deadlock rather than an inconvenience. Every nearby hostile then interrupts work at or below half health, sheltering cannot end that, and the only exit is food. Hunt refuses while a hostile is within range but has no health requirement, so take the first clear daylight moment to hunt beef, porkchop, mutton or chicken and eat, before continuing the requested work. Carrying two cooked or raw meats is what keeps a bad night from becoming a stalled day. Automatic basic melee self-defense is available; crop cultivation, PvP and ranged combat are not implemented. Do not claim hostile survival readiness from Peaceful results.

CREEPERS: a creeper within sixteen blocks that cannot be fought makes the controller sprint clear (twice at most) before sheltering, since a creeper starts swelling within three blocks whether or not it can see the agent. KEEPING WHAT YOU EARN: everything carried is dropped on death and is usually lost for good, and this agent has died repeatedly while holding all of its tools, iron and torches. A base chest is death insurance and already exists: place a chest station at the base, then deposit spare tools, ores, food and torches before any risky trip (mining below ground, exploring at night, fighting). Withdraw what the next job needs. Prefer carrying one spare tool rather than five. Progress that survives a death is worth more than progress that does not.

ARMOUR AND SHIELD: the executor automatically wears the best protective piece it carries for head, torso, legs and feet before every ordinary action, and equips a carried shield in the off hand when it fights. It never acquires them on its own, so plan for them. A full leather set is 24 leather: hunt leather from cows, then acquire leather_helmet, leather_chestplate, leather_leggings and leather_boots. Iron armour needs iron ingots from mining and smelting and is far better. A shield is six planks and one iron ingot and blunts skeleton arrows and pillager bolts. Unarmoured nights are where this agent has died most; when iron or leather is already available, arming and armouring is worth a round before mining or exploring.

DAMAGED BASE: players and explosions change the saved base, and a base whose shell, doorway approach or light is no longer intact stops counting as shelter, so retreat refuses it and the dusk rule treats the bot as homeless. The build action repairs it: build at the saved base origin sources and places only the blocks that differ from the design, clears the doorway approach and re-verifies the door. When a receipt or safety message names base damage, repair it before relying on it for the night. The observation's baseCondition is the retreat's own inspection of the saved base: usable false, with its reason, means every retreat will refuse the base until the build action repairs it.

EMERGENCY BURROW: when a threat interrupts work and no verified shelter is reachable, the controller digs three or four cells straight down through a checked column (or, when the ground cannot be dug, walls itself in with ten carried blocks). The column must be soft ground (dirt, grass, coarse dirt, podzol, rooted dirt, mud, sand, gravel, clay, moss, snow or mycelium), or stone, deepslate, granite, andesite, diorite, tuff, sandstone, cobblestone or terracotta while a pickaxe of any material is carried: without a pickaxe, rocky ground refuses the burrow with "Only N diggable cells below" and the only shelter left is a hut needing ten carried full cubes and level ground, caps the shaft with dug earth, waits underground for daylight with no hostile within twelve blocks, tunnels back to the surface and resumes the saved request once. The planner never requests this; it happens automatically, so a treeless or baseless night is not a reason to stop working or ask the player to move. In daylight a lone zombie, husk, spider or skeleton that lingers near the shaft for a minute is confronted rather than waited out, and if it cannot be struck the bot moves to open ground and continues instead of digging in again.
FIRST NIGHT: with no built and lit base (a placed torch station) the controller digs in on its own at dusk (12500 ticks) and comes out at daylight, so night work without a base is not possible; with no saved base, the enclosed starter shelter comes before every other prerequisite. From the very first planks craft a wooden axe (three planks, two sticks): it is the wood tool and the early weapon (7 damage), and self-defense only engages with a weapon in hand. Craft a wooden pickaxe (three planks, two sticks) next, before any mining goal calls for one: on rocky ground it is the difference between an emergency burrow and no shelter at all, and a night with neither a lit base nor a diggable column is spent taking damage in the open. Build the walls, roof and door from carried logs as soon as there are enough, and add lighting, a furnace and stone tools afterwards; an unlit closed shelter already stops melee mobs and creepers, while a quarry or ore trip at dusk leaves the agent exposed. Read timeOfDay: 12000 to 23000 is night.
SELF-DEFENSE: A provider-independent controller can interrupt work or following to fight one observed zombie, husk, spider or skeleton within ten blocks (a wooden axe counts as a weapon), when no additional potential threat is within eight blocks, health is at least 14, hunger at least 10, and a serviceable stone/iron/diamond/netherite axe or sword is carried. It equips the weapon, equips a carried shield when supported, spaces attacks, checks sight/range, and limits pursuit to eight blocks and 30 seconds. Sword attacks are refused near players or other bystanders. Additional enemies, low health/food, lost contact, missing equipment or a dangerous mob such as a creeper cause a shelter attempt. Stop cancels attacks and shield use. This is automatic close-range self-defense, not an arbitrary attack command or protection of the player. Prepare a serviceable weapon through prepare and acquire a shield if requested; no gear is granted automatically. After observed mob death and two clear seconds, following resumes or the interrupted request is freshly replanned, up to three combat continuations per request, unless an uncertain effect requires review. Disappearance or reconnect never counts as a kill. Do not claim universal combat capability.
Action eat: item must be an ordinary carried food (bread, apple, carrot, baked_potato, cooked meats/fish or similar safe food), count is a maximum of 1–8 portions, name="", design=[]. Stops at full hunger and requires both inventory decrement and hunger increase. It does not acquire food. Avoid poisonous, teleporting and special-effect foods. Never retry an uncertain consumption automatically.
Architecture supports floor torch cells using block="torch". Each needs full-block support directly below; placing on a barrel or crafting table is supported. Keep torches clear of declared checkpoints and door approaches. Torches are not structural supports. You can add them to an existing named project using revise without changing its other cells. Verify the resulting build; do not equate placement alone with measured interior lighting.

Action hunt: item beef, porkchop, mutton, chicken, rabbit, leather, feather, white_wool or string (Minecraft's raw meats have no raw_ prefix), or meat for food from whichever farm animal is nearest (counts every raw meat carried), count=1–8 desired total carried, name="", design=[]. The survival observation lists nearbyAnimals (farm animals within 32 blocks with distances). Prefer a listed species. With none listed the hunt walks up to six bounded cardinal legs of 32 blocks looking for animals and reports what it found, so one hunt is worth trying; if it returns with legs spent and no kill, do not retry hunt species by species, get food another way (harvest berries, crops) or move the base's work toward where animals were seen. Kills the nearest matching farm animal within 32 blocks with the best carried weapon (fists if none), collects the drops, and pauses if a hostile approaches. Raw beef, porkchop and mutton are safe to eat; raw chicken is not. Hunt when food is below 14 with nothing edible carried. String comes from spiders (0 to 2 each; a bow needs 3): hunt string only in daylight on the open surface, where spiders are calm, carrying a sword or an axe and with health 12 or more; it refuses at night and under rock, and breaks off at half health. At night a spider that closes in is fought by self-defense instead.
Action shoot (ranged combat with a bow): name="", item=an entity type (blaze, skeleton, ghast, zombie, end_crystal is not a creature and is refused), count=1, design=[], target=null. Needs a bow and arrows in the inventory. Shoots the nearest loaded entity of that type within 32 blocks with fully drawn arrows, aiming for the drop and leading a moving target, until it dies, the arrows run out or 12 shots are spent. Refuses the same targets as attack, a target with a block in the line of sight, and one out of bow range, and holds fire (cancelling the draw) while a player, villager, golem, pet, armour stand, frame, painting or piglin is within a block and a half of the arrow's path, out to twelve blocks past the target. Reports killed only on the death report; otherwise target_survived with the shots fired and arrows left. After a kill it picks up what fell within a few blocks of the body on a level it can walk to (result.collected.gained), and lists in result.collected.missed any drop it could not reach; plan collect only for those. It breaks off, failing with the health it has left, once the fight has cost health and left half or less. Use it for mobs that keep their distance or fly, such as blazes and ghasts, and from cover. Call shoot as soon as the target is listed within 32 blocks: it checks the line of sight and the range itself and says which one failed, so walking around for a sightline first wastes the shot window (lab, 10 October: the target wandered out of view while the bot repositioned).
Action sleep: name="", item="", count=0, design=[]. After dusk (world time 12541 or later) or in a thunderstorm, uses a bed already at the base or places a carried one in a clear pair of interior cells, sleeps until morning, and reports the wake time. Sleeping sets the spawn point, skips the night's dangers and clears the phantom timer that sends winged mobs after three sleepless days. It refuses with a hostile within eight blocks, so clear or shelter first. A bed is three wool and three planks: hunt white_wool from sheep, then acquire white_bed. Prefer sleeping to waiting out the night underground whenever a bed is available.
Action harvest: item="sweet_berries", count=1–32 desired total carried, name="", design=[]. Harvests ripe loaded sweet berry bushes within 48 blocks through activation, preserves them at age 1 for regrowth, and confirms inventory pickup from adjacent safe ground. Stops rather than destroying bushes or waiting indefinitely for growth. Use berries as an initial renewable food reserve; acquire does not implement berry harvesting.
Action use: put one carried item to work on one named block cell, the way a player right-clicks with something in hand. item is the carried item ID, count is 1, design=[], and target is {x,y,z,how,dimension}. dimension must match survival.dimension: a coordinate means nothing in the wrong world, and the executor refuses before sending anything. It never acquires the item; acquire first. One use per step, and a plan carries at most eight steps, so a long sequence spans planning rounds.
how="click" clicks a block. Name the cell where the EFFECT must land, not a tidy block nearby. If that cell is empty the executor clicks the neighbour on the face pointing into it, so the effect appears where you named: fire from flint_and_steel inside a portal frame, a seed or sapling on the ground below. If that cell already holds a block, it clicks that block: an ender_eye into one specific end_portal_frame, bone_meal on a crop, shears on a beehive.
how="point" looks at the cell and uses the item without clicking, and is refused for anything but a bucket. A bucket cannot be clicked onto a block, and a water or lava source has no clickable shape; the server decides where the effect lands, so a pointed use must leave name empty.
name is the block you expect at the effect cell afterwards, written as a block ID with optional states: "nether_portal", "wheat", "end_portal_frame[eye=true]". Declare it whenever you can. If it is already true the executor reports already_satisfied and spends nothing, so re-planning never burns a second eye of ender. If it is false afterwards the step FAILS and names what it observed: lighting a frame that reads back "fire" means the obsidian frame is incomplete or the wrong shape. Leave name empty when the proof is not a block: filling a bucket, bone-mealing a crop.
Every use also records the spend, the carried count or tool durability the server charged. A use whose effect cannot be read but which WAS charged returns a partial result saying exactly that. A use that is neither observed nor charged fails and names the block it aimed at. The executor refuses a target in an unloaded chunk and says how far to travel, refuses a cell inside a saved project except the checked portal ignition described below, refuses when the item is not carried, and refuses a target that opens a container. Full solid blocks, doors, beds, stairs, torches and workstations are refused by name: build, construct and place own those and carry their site, orientation and lighting checks. Never repeat an unconfirmed use unchanged.
observation.interactables lists loaded obsidian, end_portal_frame, nether_portal, end_portal and farmland within 32 blocks with their block states, plus any block your goal's block_at criteria name, plus the nearest water and lava SOURCE cell (level=0), which is the only kind a bucket can fill from. Absence there is local evidence only, and a truncated flag means there were more.
Action throw_eye: name="", item="ender_eye", count=1, design=[], target=null. Overworld only, under open sky, with an eye of ender carried. Throws one eye, watches it fly, and picks it up where it falls. The receipt says heading, a compass bearing in degrees clockwise from north (0 is toward -z, 90 toward +x, 180 toward +z, 270 toward -x); travelled, the blocks it flew; overhead true when it sank within 6 blocks, meaning the stronghold is underneath, so dig down there; and recovered or shattered (about one eye in five breaks). Each bearing is saved in places.stronghold_bearing; the newest entry's estimate {x,z} is where it crosses an earlier one, or estimateRefused says why not. Throw once, walk a few hundred blocks across the heading, throw again: bearings under 10 degrees apart do not cross usefully. Then follow the estimate and throw again near it until one is overhead. A partial receipt means an eye was spent and no flight seen: it is no bearing.
Action stand: name="", item="ender_pearl", count=1–8 pearls to gain, design=[], target=null. Ender pearls from endermen, the Nether only, on a kitted trip (bow, 16 arrows, an iron sword or axe, iron armour with golden boots, health 16 or more) with an enderman within 24 blocks and blocks to wall in. It walls in where it stands in a 2-high hut (an enderman is 2.9 tall and cannot get in), opens a port toward the nearest enderman, looks it in the eye to provoke it, strikes it through the port when it teleports there, picks up a pearl that falls within one step of the port, and closes the port between kills and whenever a second enderman comes near. Under 16 health it shuts the port and heals before the next. It stops at the count, under 12 health or after 10 minutes. The receipt says kills, pearls (counted from the inventory), ports and stopped; closed false with portOpen means a mob kept the port from closing, so wall in or move before anything else. Kills with no pearl fail with the reason. A warped forest has the most endermen and little else hostile: stand there. Use it instead of attack enderman, which chases one into the open.
Action bore: name="", item="", count=1–64 columns, design=[], target={x,y,z}. The Nether only. A covered one-wide, two-high tunnel toward that cell, the way through rock to a blaze spawner when hostiles hold the open ground. The path is Manhattan: the longer horizontal axis first in full, then the other, never a diagonal step, the height changing at most one block per column. Each column is dug from the one behind it, and every opening it uncovers beside, above or under the new cells is plugged with carried blocks before the bot steps in, so it never stands in the open. Needs a pickaxe and 8 carried blocks (the dug netherrack refills them); across open ground with a solid floor (a fortress bridge) it builds the tunnel's walls and roof from carried blocks, up to five a column, so bring two stacks of cobblestone, and the receipt's covered counts those columns. It refuses what dig refuses and stops in the column before, naming the cell: anything not natural ground (nether bricks are the fortress: go round them), saved project cells, falling blocks overhead, and lava beside, above or under a column (bore again toward a point three blocks to the side). It stops by itself one column short of open space at its own level and keeps that column as the wall; beyond names the open cell. The slit below opens only when that wall is within 6 blocks of the target; open space farther off stops it with the wall whole and the reason says how far short, so bore around it (from a room wall with nothing dug, step to another wall or re-aim). With health 16 or more, not burning and food 6 or more it opens a three-cell slit at head height in that wall (the wall's lower cell stays, so nothing walks in), otherwise the reason says what it waits for and a later bore from the same cell opens it. A wall a step above or below the bot gets no slit: bore a level leg toward a target at the bot's own y first. Magma under a column stops it like lava. A bore longer than ten minutes stops at a column and says timed out. It is re-entrant: after an alarm or a stop, bore again from where the bot stands. The receipt gives columns, to, stoppedAt, reason, plugged, slit, beyond and wall; zero columns and no slit is a failure, whatever it plugged.
Action make_obsidian: name="", item="", count=1–64 total obsidian to carry, design=[], target null. Needs a carried diamond or netherite pickaxe and a water bucket, or an empty bucket and an open water source within 32 blocks, which it fills first. It finds a lava SOURCE open to the air within 64 blocks with a solid rim block beside it, stands one cell beyond the rim where no lava touches the body, pours onto the rim's top so the water runs over the pool, takes the water back, then mines and collects the obsidian that has no lava or water beside it. When the only lava in view is sealed inside rock it opens it itself: it tunnels to a dry standing cell two blocks out, digs only the layer above the lava (never at the lava's level, never where liquid touches the dug block), and then pours as above; up to three openings a trip. When no lava is in view at all it walks up to six search legs of 48 blocks and surveys after each. Tunnelling spends plain pickaxes (the diamond one is kept for the obsidian): started above ground with fewer than 64 plain pickaxe uses it first makes three stone pickaxes and packs six sticks and a crafting table, gathering wood if it must; under rock it makes more from carried sticks and cobblestone, and with neither it cannot, so before sending the bot deep with worn plain pickaxes and no sticks, prepare stone_pickaxe and carry sticks while wood and a table are in reach. So call make_obsidian directly, from wherever the bot is (underground is fine): do not dig down or search for lava with goto first, and do not abandon it because an earlier attempt saw only sealed lava. Flowing lava turns to cobblestone, not obsidian. It tries other rims, up to eight pours. It returns partial with the reason when the lava in reach runs out before the total, and fails with the reason when it makes none; a failure counts the lava it saw, names the nearest, and says which rule turned each source away, so read it before moving. Its failure reason says how much lava it saw, how many sealed pockets it tried to open and why each was refused, and how far it searched; only after such a failure is moving to a new area (at least 48 blocks, or much deeper, toward lava it saw) worth a step before calling it again. In most worlds lava lakes lie in caves and near the deep lava level around y=-54. It never opens a sealed lake for water. Use it for obsidian instead of composing goto and use steps: those cannot tell a source from flowing water and do not keep the bot off a pool's edge.
FIRE: burning or standing in lava interrupts all work at once. The controller gets out of the lava, pours a carried water bucket at its feet or steps into nearby water, and continues the saved request when the fire is out. Keep a water bucket whenever you work near lava; without one a burning bot can only wait the fire out, at about one health a second. A goto destination with lava beside it is refused, and every walk pays extra to pass a cell next to lava.
PORTALS: walks never step into a portal cell, and standing in one interrupts everything to step straight out, because four seconds inside carries a player through. Light a portal from outside its frame, and leave the frame's interior clear. The one way through is the use_portal action, and from the Overworld only when observation.portalTravel.nether is "on"; when it is "off" never plan use_portal there and never try to reach the Nether another way. From the Nether use_portal always goes home. The End has its own switch: from the Overworld use_portal takes the nearer lit portal within 48 blocks, and an end portal only when observation.portalTravel.end is "on"; when it is "off" never plan use_portal beside one and never try to reach the End another way. Crossing, the bot steps onto the end portal and lands on the obsidian platform, digging out through end stone if it is buried (never the obsidian); projects.portal_link records the stronghold portal and the platform. The End has no day or night (ignore the clock), and every edge of its island is the void: never run outward; away from danger is toward 0,0. From the End use_portal goes home only through the exit fountain at 0,0, which lights when the dragon dies.
THE NETHER: survival.dimension says where the bot is. projects.portal_link records each crossing as a pair of portals; the way home is the Nether end of the pair the bot came through. places.nether_portal is the Overworld portal the bot lit, saved when the lighting is observed: return nether_portal walks back to it, and a second portal is not built while that one stands. In the Nether there is no day or night (the world clock still runs, ignore it), no water (a bucket evaporates, so fire is walked out of and make_obsidian refuses), and no bed (it explodes). The saved base, its stations and saved places stay in the Overworld: retreat, station actions and walks to them refuse there, so shelter is a burrow in netherrack or a cobblestone hut, which ghast fireballs cannot break. Never mine obsidian there. Zombified piglins are neutral and attack refuses them, because one hit turns every one nearby; piglins attack anyone without a gold armour piece, so carry golden boots, which the bot wears on arrival. Lava lies everywhere, often under a thin floor: keep walks short and in sight of the portal until the trip is planned. Hunting blazes, stay within 16 blocks of the arrival portal and let them come to you: shoot those in sight from beside it, and never walk or dig further into a fortress to chase one (lab run 107 did, and died fifty blocks out with two rods in its pack). A blaze spawner the request names, or one already known, is a trip and not a chase: go straight to it, and when hostiles hold the open ground at the arrival portal (a wither skeleton, hoglins, blazes: lab run 142, seven crossings, never more than thirteen blocks of walkway), do not walk the walkways at all: reach the spawner with bore, not dig: a covered tunnel through netherrack, up to 64 columns a step, that stops by itself one column short of the open room and opens a slit there to shoot through (never through nether bricks, never over lava; when it stops early it says why, so plan the next leg from there). Stop on solid floor with the spawner in sight 8 to 12 blocks off, and shoot the blazes it makes from there, picking up rods between waves. On the trip with the kit (a bow, arrows, iron armour, an iron sword) a hostile the bot can fight is fought where it stands and cover is built where it stands; the portal is for going home hurt or with the rods, not for every hostile. Going home, use_portal walks back the trail of ground the bot came by, so take one route there and do not wander off it. Near the portal the portal room and the way home are both a step away.
Flint comes from gravel: acquire flint, or flint_and_steel, mines exposed gravel and keeps the gravel it digs on the way. Expect about ten gravel per flint.

Action retreat, name="base", count=0, item="", design=[] enters the saved base after checking its shell, access and conservative lighting, closes its wooden door and confirms interior arrival. It does not repair damage. A provider-independent supervisor checks health events and once per second, pauses work at health <=10, food <=6, or a potential hostile within ten blocks outside Peaceful. A threat is evaluated for the bounded self-defense behavior above first. When fighting is unsuitable or fails, it tries the primary base followed by up to three nearby completed named buildings within a total two-minute recovery budget, and eats carried safe food if hungry. Fallback candidates must have one hand-operated door, a live enclosed room, supported clear access, sufficient conservative lighting and no observed potential hostile occupant. Rejected candidates and the selected shelter are recorded in safety.attempts and safety.retreat. This does not replace the primary base or its storage. Ordinary work remains paused for explicit Resume, except the first eligible threat interruption can resume after a verified clear shelter watch. Stop cancels recovery too. This is an attempted retreat, not a guarantee of safety from combat or blocked routes.

Action tunnel_out has the same empty item/count/design and name="base" as retreat. It opens the base's back exit: from inside the base, with a pickaxe, it digs the reserved interior floor cell straight down through checked rock to nine blocks, sealing any air beside the shaft with carried blocks, and leaves you standing at the bottom with the base closed above you. It refuses outside the base, without a pickaxe, over liquid, through a container or bedrock, or when the shaft would open into a cave; shallower than four blocks it climbs back and reports why. Use it when the door is refused for night or for a hostile inside its interruption radius and the work is underground (ore, stone, prospecting, obsidian); the refusal names it. observation.backExit reports the shaft (cell, bottom, open) and atFoot=true when you stand at its bottom. At the foot you are inside your base for safety and already underground: do not retreat or leave_base first; work from there with prospect, goto with dig, dig and acquire, which start at the shaft. retreat from anywhere in the shaft climbs back up, keeps the door shut and closes the shaft; the next tunnel_out reopens it.

Action leave_base has the same empty item/count/design and name="base" as retreat. It opens the occupied verified fallback shelter door when inside one; otherwise it uses the primary base door. It exits to the outside approach and closes the door again. Use it before gathering or travel when sheltered inside either the base or a fallback building. From 11500 ticks until daylight, and while a hostile stands inside its interruption radius of the base, the door is refused with a reason; underground work then goes by tunnel_out. The executor also exits an occupied verified fallback before movement-dependent work if the model omits leave_base, recording that departure. Ordinary pathfinding does not open arbitrary closed doors. Morning departure clearance checks the first three approach blocks: a nearby mob on that corridor blocks departure, while a classified mob elsewhere in the observed 24-block area is recorded as peripheral and still requires re-observation. Unknown-position mobs remain blocking. This corridor check does not establish a safe route beyond the approach or combat readiness.
These capabilities are available to all selected model providers. For goal formulation, use inventory for the requested food reserve, sheltered (target="base", count=1, item="") for current-request retreat plus live interior/closed-door/light checks, and review for preserving/regrowing bushes. Always include sheltered when the user asks to finish inside the closed base. Do not use the shelter check (which requires a build action) for retreat. General crop planting/cultivation remains unavailable; harvesting existing ripe berry bushes is supported.

Food-source evidence: foraging reports the executor's current loaded berry-bush scan, including positions, ages and ripeCount. Unknown observations do not prove absence. Zero ripe bushes gives no harvest target in that scan; do not repeat the same failed harvest without a newly observed ripe source. Only observed unripe bushes support a regrowth expectation. Use survival.nearbyAnimals for observed hunting candidates; after a completed empty search, consider another observed source or bounded movement into a different area. A shelter exit or automatic continuation preserves earlier search failures.

CURRENT EXECUTOR AND HISTORICAL FAILURES: these capability instructions describe the running executor. Prior assistant messages, request summaries and retrieved lessons are historical evidence, not current capability rules. Experience marked stale (including unknown executor provenance) may describe behavior fixed since it was recorded. Preserve that evidence, but do not convert a currently supported, machine-checkable requested outcome into review-only work because of an old refusal. Re-observe prerequisites and permit a bounded retry under the current executor guards when the capability changed. Current unsafe conditions, uncertain charged uses, explicit user constraints and unchanged current-executor failures still govern; a revision change is not permission to bypass them.
PORTAL FRAMES: build a Nether portal frame with its four corner cells filled with a cheap solid block such as cobblestone. The portal ignores the corners, and without them the top row has nothing to be placed against, so construct stops short (lab run 66: 17 of 25 placed, no adjacent placement face for the top row). PROTECTED PORTAL IGNITION: use may ignite a saved project's standard obsidian frame in the Overworld. Carry flint_and_steel, set name="nether_portal", count=1 and target.how="click", and target one of the two bottom interior air cells. The executor must see a complete ten-obsidian perimeter enclosing a clear two-wide, three-high interior, in either horizontal orientation; it rechecks immediately before use. Incomplete, obstructed or larger protected frames remain refused. Keep activation as a block_at criterion, with a use receipt and live block verification; do not replace it with a review criterion. Light from outside, then stop outside. Lighting never crosses: crossing is only ever use_portal, under the PORTALS rule.

# Survival capability contract

The existing Paper lab remains in Survival gamemode and Peaceful difficulty. No mob-combat or continuous hostile-survival acceptance is implied by these tests.

## Actions

- `harvest`: item `sweet_berries`, desired carried total 1–32. Finds ripe loaded bushes within 48 blocks, activates without breaking, checks reset age and inventory pickup, and leaves plants for regrowth. New berry drops can be followed to safe landing cells downhill. Unreachable bushes are skipped within a bounded action. This is wild harvesting, not crop planting or a managed farm.
- `retreat`: target `base`. Checks the saved shell, actual centre approach, interior floor/headroom and a conservative block-light lower bound of at least eight. Enters through the wooden door and verifies closed-door interior arrival. No repair, demolition or new shelter construction occurs during recovery.
- `leave_base`: verifies the same base, opens its door, exits and closes it again.
- `eat`: ordinary safe carried food, at most eight portions. Stops at full hunger, requires both hunger increase and inventory decrement, preserves its cap across restart and blocks uncertain replay.

Goal formulation and action planning share `src/companion/survival-capabilities.txt`. The `sheltered` completion criterion requires a fresh current-request retreat receipt plus live interior, door, shell and lighting checks. A review note cannot substitute for this executable requirement.

## Supervisor

Health events and one-second checks trigger recovery at health <=10, food <=6, or an observed potential hostile within ten blocks outside Peaceful. Low health interrupts work only when something can be done about it: a hostile within sixteen blocks to escape, or nightfall with a verified lit base the bot is not already inside. Neither the health rule nor the threat rule fires at all while the bot is verifiably inside its closed lit base: the shelter is already the answer, and interrupting there only retreats into the room the bot is standing in, which had paused a brand-new request within two seconds of receiving it. A health retreat now arms the same watch-and-continue as a threat retreat, so a hurt evening no longer ends the request until an operator resumes it. A bot resting in its own lit base with no carried food never heals, because hunger under eighteen stops natural regeneration, so interrupting it there trapped it in an endless retreat-and-pause loop at five health; the planner is now free to go and find food. Conditional mobs alone do not trigger proximity recovery. Coverage is limited to loaded entities; it does not prove aggression or line of sight.

The provider-independent controller interrupts the request, stops current work, waits for the old skill to unwind and for connection/chunk readiness, attempts retreat and eats carried safe food if hungry after arrival. Work stays paused for explicit Resume. Stop cancels recovery and suppresses further recovery until a new request. Missing shelter, blocked access, timeout or death is a failure, never a safety claim. Restart does not silently replay recovery.

Active-skill cancellation keeps the connection open. Stop clears movement, stops digging and item use, and marks the skill cancelled. The controller waits up to 20 seconds for the native operation and its cleanup to finish before dispatching another skill or follow command. Existing checks stop later skill steps and guarded crafting clicks; a native interaction already in flight can finish. Digging that starts late after an awaited turn is cancelled on the next physics tick. Ordinary movement settings are restored after cancellation. A stuck operation blocks the handoff explicitly rather than permitting overlapping work. This is cooperative cancellation, not rollback of an already sent action.

The live snapshot includes a connection ID so a reconnect cannot hide between polling samples. Lifecycle and cancellation events are recorded in the backend log. Unexpected network loss still uses the existing reconnect behavior. Actual hungry eating and a separate food-replenishment request now have live acceptance below. A single active-husk avoidance case now passes below; broader hostile encounters and full day/night survival remain unverified.

## Bounded observation-based walking recovery

World skills now share a route-recovery wrapper for ordinary walking. Retreat bypasses the wait so emergency shelter selection can immediately try another candidate. An initial walking attempt retains its 15-second cap and the task's existing movement policy. After a no-path, search-timeout or unconfirmed-arrival failure, it clears movement and watches for up to 30 seconds. A nearby collision/hazard change within ten blocks of the starting or current position, or a changed grounded feet cell, permits exactly one fresh walking attempt. No relevant change preserves the original failure; a failed retry stops. Other errors bypass recovery. It does not replay crafting, digging, transfers or consumption, widen drop limits, edit terrain or resume a paused user request. Existing task/safety deadlines still apply.

Before that wait, two bounded self-help steps run under the same walking policy. A bot wedged in a slot steps toward any open neighbour, flat or one up. A bot at the bottom of a pit (its own prospecting shaft, collapsed sand, a creeper crater) judges the open column above it and the first height, up to eight, with a solid ledge beside it; with enough carried full-cube non-gravity blocks it pillars up with the burrow climb, steps onto the ledge and retries once. Roofed columns are never dug, a stalled climb keeps the original route error, and the retreat kind still bypasses recovery. Receipts carry the trigger (`stepped_free`, `climbed_free`).

## Daylight judgement against a lone mob

Two rules keep a lone zombie, husk, spider or skeleton from holding the bot underground or in a dig-in loop. First, the burrow wait confronts instead of waiting: in daylight, with health at the mob's defense margin and food of at least ten, a single such mob that lingers within 16 blocks for 60 seconds clears the wait, the bot digs out and the ordinary threat interruption runs self-defense. Creepers, pairs of mobs, low health or hunger keep the bot dug in as before. Second, when self-defense is rejected or fails in daylight against a lone such mob (roofed, behind a wall, kiting), the bot sprints 24 blocks to open ground instead of digging in, then watches for ten quiet seconds with no potential hostile within 16 blocks and continues the request as an open-ground safety continuation. A mob that follows sends the bot on to the shelter chain. A bot that has already sprinted clear this episode, typically from a creeper, and finds nothing within 16 blocks in daylight takes the same open-ground watch and continues the request from where it stands, rather than walking all the way back to base. A Resume issued while the bot is dug in or walled in never plans from underground: it marks the shelter wait as an explicit continuation (which also bypasses the idle-continuation cap) or re-arms a wait that timed out, and the request continues after the exit. The pit climb digs through one full non-gravity ceiling block with open air above it, such as its own burrow cap.

When every shelter is refused while a threat is near, the bot never stands still. It runs the `climb` safety action (the pit climb as a runner task), sprints 24 blocks from the nearest potential hostile, and tries the ground there once more (dig in, then wall in). A flight that moves fewer than two blocks is a failure, not a receipt. A hut whose walls already stand, as in a one-block shaft, needs no ground under those walls and places only the roof. Receipts record the `move` attempt beside the shelter attempts.

A torch station is recorded at the start of its placement with `confirmed: false`; only a confirmed torch makes the base count as shelter for the dusk and health rules. Three torch placements in the fresh world were cancelled by safety interruptions, which left an unconfirmed record that suppressed the dusk dig-in and sent the bot walking to an unlit base at night, where it was shot to half health. The light check accepts `torch` and `wall_torch`. The survival observation now lists `nearbyAnimals` (farm animals within 32 blocks) so the planner hunts only what is there; three consecutive hunts on the beach (cow, pig, sheep) had each ended with nothing in range. A hunt with nothing in sight now walks up to six bounded cardinal legs of 32 blocks to look, growing its boundary with each ring and capped at three minutes, then reports the legs spent, the animals it saw and where it ended. The search walk never sprints, since sprinting burns the hunger the hunt exists to restore; only the chase does.

## Sleeping

A `sleep` action removes the night rather than surviving it. After dusk (world time 12541 or later) or in a thunderstorm it uses a bed already within sixteen blocks, or places a carried one in two clear interior cells of the base chosen away from the doorway approach and from every recorded station, then sleeps until the server wakes the bot at dawn and confirms the clock reached morning. It refuses plainly when it is daylight, when a potential hostile is within eight blocks, when no bed is carried or placed, or when no clear pair of interior cells exists. A slept night sets the spawn point and clears the phantom timer; three sleepless days had been sending phantoms at the bot, which cost two retreats in one night. A bed is three wool and three planks, so `white_wool` is now a huntable sheep drop.

A `slept` completion criterion verifies a night actually slept during the request, from the sleep receipt and an observed morning wake. It exists because a contract can otherwise be written entirely out of `review` criteria, which are never machine-checked: asked for wool, a bed and a night's sleep, the planner produced three review criteria, the runtime found nothing executable to check, and the request reported "the executable checks passed" after the bot walked into its base and did none of the work. A task contract of review criteria alone is now rejected.

## Standing off instead of looping

Evidence from a family session on 12 September: over thirteen minutes the agent produced fifty-seven messages against three operator instructions, and two of those three instructions never ran at all. It had died fourteen times, so it carried no pickaxe and no blocks; the players had put a birch log in the base doorway, mined a shell block to air and let a spider inside. Every shelter attempt therefore failed, and the failure repeated: nine low-health interruptions, nine dig-in attempts, eight wall-in attempts, four identical recovery failures.

A failed recovery now records a fingerprint of everything that could change its outcome: whether a pickaxe is carried, how many placeable blocks are carried, whether the base is inside/usable/unusable, whether a hostile is within sixteen blocks, and coarse health and carried-food state. While that fingerprint is unchanged the chain does not run again, so it neither repeats itself nor preempts what the operator types. It re-arms the moment anything relevant changes, and any new instruction clears it outright. The final message names each blocker and what would clear it, for example: the base is not usable (doorway obstructed by birch_log); I have no pickaxe to dig in; I have no blocks to wall in with. I need a pickaxe or ten building blocks.

Gathering had the matching problem: it explored only when nothing was in sight, never when everything in sight was unreachable, so twelve fenced-off or built-over trees ended the job at four logs of twelve. Twelve unreachable targets now send the bot to look somewhere else, up to its existing eight-leg budget, and the blocker names how many places it looked from.
